Why AI

AI lets a small studio make real software.

Building a game or an app used to take a team: programmers, artists, testers, months of work. We don't have those resources, and without AI this studio would not exist.

We see AI as a democratization of making software. Ideas that would have stayed in a notebook can now become finished products that people use.

How we work

A person defines it. AI builds it. Both check it.

This is the way we build every product. We think of it as a new way of making software.

  1. 1Person

    A precise brief

    A person decides what gets made and why, who it is for, how it should feel and which rules it must keep. Every rule is written down, so nothing is left for the AI to guess.

  2. 2AI

    Execution

    AI writes the code line by line and much of the graphics pixel by pixel, following the brief. It does the work that used to need a team.

  3. 3Person + AI

    Review by both

    Automated tests, AI reviews and bots that play the games check the result. Then a person tests it by hand on a real phone. It ships only when both agree.

When the review finds a problem, the work goes back to step 1: we fix the brief first, then the product.

Where the saved time goes

We don't type every line. We spend that time on the product.

  • Product designWhat the product is for, and what it should leave out.
  • Logic and rulesGame rules, edge cases and numbers that add up.
  • QualityAccessibility, clear texts in every language, no shortcuts in the code.
  • User experienceBig touch targets, readable text, a flow that makes sense the first time.
  • TestingAutomated checks first, then hands on real devices.

The limits of AI

We know what AI gets wrong.

AI can produce work that looks finished but is careless, generic or simply wrong. That is AI slop, and avoiding it is the reason we work the way we do.

A person owns every decisionAI never decides what a product is. When something is unclear, it asks.
Facts get checkedOur educational games teach real physics and real maths. A person reviews every fact.
Nothing ships untestedIf a feature has no test, it isn't finished.
We throw things awayIdeas and designs that don't meet the bar get dropped, however much work went into them.

Why "Random Idea"

A maths game for kids, a family organizer and an arcade about a drop of water. From the outside, it looks like we pick our next project at random.

We have no single product line and no single genre, and the name is a joke at our own expense. In reality, every project starts with a reason.

NumBeasts exists because our developer's children needed to practise maths. Second Shift Manager exists because none of the family organizers we tried did what we needed. Steamy exists because our developer always wanted to play a game like it, and nobody had made one.
